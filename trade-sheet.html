The three trades, and exactly how to place them

All three are the same shape: an iron condor sold at the expected-move band on a cash-settled European index option, held to expiry with no management. They differ only in how far out the short strikes sit and how long you hold. Measured on 2,688 real sessions, January 2016 to September 2026, at every possible start date.

STEP 1
Measure the move

On the morning you trade, take the index level and the VIX close. The one-sigma move is index × (VIX ÷ 100) × √(days ÷ 365), using calendar days to expiry.

STEP 2
Set four strikes

Short strikes at the multiple of that move shown on the card. Long strikes half a sigma further out on each side. Round to the nearest listed strike.

STEP 3
Send it as one order

All four legs, one expiry, as a single iron condor at a net credit limit. Never leg in. Start at the mid and work down; if you cannot fill above the floor on the card, cancel and skip the month.

STEP 4
Size it

Max loss is the wing width minus the credit, and you know it exactly at entry. Buy the number of contracts that makes it 5 per cent of your account. Not margin, not notional.

STEP 5
Then do nothing

Hold to expiry. No stop, no adjustment, no profit target. Cash settles automatically. Hold-to-expiry is the only rule the decade actually measures.

Why you can do nothing for a month. The wings mean the worst case is posted on day one and no gap, halt or margin call can exceed it. The naked version of this trade earns more on paper and an account funded at its margin is closed out on 9 March 2020, fourteen days before the expiry that books the loss. Sizing note. One SPX condor risks about the price of a small car, so at 5 per cent that needs a large account. XSP is the same index at one tenth the size and settles the same way; every figure here divides by ten. This is research, not advice. Every premium is a model's, not a quote, which is why step 3 has a floor.
