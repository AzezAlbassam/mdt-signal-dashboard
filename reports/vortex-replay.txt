When a window first reaches 20 candles every contact on it is new at once, so a quarter or more of
all signals land on that one bar, early in the swing. A signal never lands in the bounce between D
and its confirmation (no new extreme prints there), while null A does. Held with the swing to its
confirmed end, an early entry is worth more than a random one in the same — hindsight-delimited —
window whatever the indicator says. Null C holds the phase fixed and asks only whether the contact
picks better bars than its neighbours in the same stretch of the same swing. It removes most of the
timing advantage, not all: inside its phase a signal still sits early (in-phase rank: mean position
of later signals among their phase's update bars, 0 = first, 1 = last, 0.5 = no timing bias). That
residue, not information in the walls, is the likeliest source of the small with-trend leftovers.

━━━ READING ━━━

Hindsight vs real time differ in three ways, all visible above:
 1. Position. e is pinned to 0 at D and contacts are ignored within 3% of D, so the candle being
    printed can never carry a contact. In real time D is the running extreme, so a contact on a
    pullback is beyond D and is not drawn at all; it can only appear later, once a new extreme
    makes it part of the window — that is the lag in (b).
 2. Size. W is the largest deviation in the window. Every new extreme re-sizes the walls and
    moves the vector, so contacts appear, move and vanish as the leg grows — the repaint in (c).
 3. Price. The list prints the peak candle's OHLC4. A trader acting on the screen gets the close of
    the bar on which the contact first appears — the entry give-up in (b) — and learns D only when
    the swing is confirmed over, a threshold-sized reversal later.
 4. Net. Under the exits the list implies ("video"), its contacts win 69%–85% as listed and
