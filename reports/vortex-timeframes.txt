DEFAULTS (ATR 14, threshold 4·ATR/close, K = 5, entry band 0.85 ≤ |e| < 1.25, stop |e| ≥ 1.25,
skip 3 %, N ≥ 8). Nothing was tuned. Entries and exits on bar closes, one position at a time,
one entry per projection. Costs per round trip: 0.02 % (indices, gold, TASI), 0.10 % (BTC).
Intervals: Wilson 95 % for win rates; percentile bootstrap 95 % (5000 resamples) for expectancy.
Nulls: 5000 seeded draws each, one-sided p = (#null ≥ observed + 1)/(draws + 1).

VERDICT RULE (fixed in the script header before the first run): a timeframe has an edge if,
pooled across the five markets, (1) the expectancy CI lies above 0 and (2) Holm-adjusted (over the
4 timeframes) max(pA, pB, pC) < 0.05 — it beats random direction, random bars in the same
projection, AND a matched Bollinger fade. Most accurate = the qualifying timeframe with the
highest CI lower bound; if none qualifies, the answer is "none". Win rate plays no part.

The stop (|e| ≥ 1.25) and the target (e back to 0) are levels in e, measured against the entry
projection's vector, which keeps moving toward the projected D, inside an ellipse that widens to
mid-window. Neither is a fixed price: a "stop" can close a winner and a "target" a loser, and a loss
is not capped in price — the worst trade, BTC 1D, a short entered at e = +1.07 on 2013-10-07, was held 15 bars
while the vector moved with price, and closed (stop) at -53.7%.

mkts +/n = markets with positive expectancy / markets with trades. The pooled bootstrap treats trades
as independent across markets; SPX and NDX move together, so the pooled intervals are, if anything,
too narrow. Holm(4) adjusts "all" across the four pooled timeframes (the verdict family).

 1. Live, the signals win 38%–48% of the time on every timeframe, against break-even rates of
    42%–53%. No timeframe's expectancy is distinguishable from zero in its favour, none beats
    random direction or random bars in the same projection, and 4h loses money with a CI below 0.
 2. Trades are short: a median of 2.0 bars (0.7 days) on 4h, 2.0 bars (3.0 days) on 1D, 3.0 bars (21.0 days) on 1W, 3.0 bars (91.0 days) on 1M.
    Of all 1027 closed trades, 672 (65%) hit the stop and 300 (29%) reached the vector:
    entries lie at 0.85 ≤ |e| < 1.25, so the stop is at most 0.40 e-units away and the vector at least 0.85.
 3. Why the live version has nothing to fade: when a projection is drawn, a median 25%–51% of its
    projected swing has already gone by (the pivot needs a 4·ATR reversal to be confirmed), and the
    projected walls are lopsided (median Wup/Wdn 0.62–2.58 on cells with ≥ 20 projections), so "at the wall"
    is mostly a statement about the median of past swings, not about this one.
 4. Monthly bars: 4·ATR/close is 23% SPX, 30% NDX, 27% Gold, 99% BTC, 26% TASI, so the zigzag almost
    never turns. NDX and BTC monthly never draw a projection at all; weekly BTC draws only 4.
    A monthly table would show a handful of trades in decades.
 5. The one Holm-significant cell, TASI 1D against Bollinger, beats the Bollinger fade because
    that fade lost (-1.53% per trade), not because the vortex earned: its own expectancy is
    +0.058% [-0.371%, +0.480%] and it does not beat nulls (a) or (b).
 6. In hindsight the same pivots give the video's picture — 76% / 78% / 78% / 79% wins on
    4h / 1D / 1W / 1M — because D, the walls and the contacts are all measured on the finished swing.
    The live column is what a chart would actually have shown at each close.
