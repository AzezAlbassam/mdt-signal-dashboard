Question one

Can you buy a call or a put when price breaks the band?

  • 576 combinations searched. Eight entry rules, both directions, four targets, three stops, three time limits.
  • Entry is the open of the next session — never the close that fired the signal, because that is a fill you did not have.
  • A bar that touches both your target and your stop is scored as the stop. A daily bar cannot say which came first, and assuming the good one is how a backtest lies.
  • The law underneath it all: on a path with no edge, a target T and a stop S are hit first with probability S/(T+S) and T/(T+S).

Three controls, each stricter than the last. The third one decides it.

The answer, and it is not close

No.

Search 576 ways and about 29 will look significant at a 5% threshold by construction. The real search underperformed the average noise search. The band forecasts the size of a move and says nothing about its direction.

Question two

Can you sell one? Yes — and this is the whole trade.

Call spread at 1.25σ

SELL one call1.25σ above the market BUY one call0.5σ further up Expiry30 calendar days Managementnone — hold to expiry How oftenonce a month, 12 a year

Two options. Same type. One order.

No condor. No four legs. No adjustment, no stop, no profit target.

You send it, and then you do nothing for a month. It settles in cash by itself — there is no assignment and nothing to close.

One sigma = index × (VIX ÷ 100) × √(calendar days ÷ 365). No entry filter improves it: VIX high, VIX low, nothing. The edge is in the calendar, not a signal.

That gap — 10.4 points — is the entire edge, and it is the only thing you are paid for. Loses the full maximum in 0.4% of months, about once every two and a half years.

The part nobody wants to hear

You cannot have a high win rate and a high reward.

When it wins+6.4% of risk When it loses−40.9% of risk Reward to risk0.16 One loss takes backsix wins

Win rate and reward are welded together. A 90% win rate forces a reward near 1-to-9. A 3-to-1 reward forces a win rate near a quarter.

There is no trade anywhere in this study with both high at once. Anyone offering you one is selling something.

This is the same arithmetic that killed question one, seen from the other side. Buying gives you 3-to-1 and a 44% win rate — and no edge. Selling gives you a 97% win rate and 0.16 — and a real one.

A backtest that settles every trade on the terminal close cannot see a margin call. The account is gone before the trade it books ever expires. That is why it is measured separately rather than assumed away.

How you actually do it

Five steps, twelve times a year.

STEP 1
Pick a day

Once a month, the same day each month. There is no setup to wait for.

STEP 2
Measure the move

One sigma is index × (VIX ÷ 100) × √(days ÷ 365), using calendar days to expiry.

STEP 3
Two strikes

Sell the call 1.25σ above. Buy the call 0.5σ further up. One order, net credit limit. Never leg in.

STEP 4
Size it

Max loss is the strike gap minus the credit. Buy the contracts that make it 5% of your account.

STEP 5
Do nothing

You never sell. Hold to expiry. No stop, no adjustment, no target. It cash-settles by itself.

The fill floor. The model collects about 11% of the strike gap as credit. If you cannot fill above 5.6%, there is no edge left — cancel and skip the month.

What this is, and what it is not

Eleven years is eleven years. It is not a guarantee.

  • Every premium here is a model's price, not a broker's quote. That is why step 3 has a floor.
  • The measured edge is 10.4 points of win rate over what the payoff demands. It is real, and it is not large.
  • One SPX spread risks real money. XSP is the same index at one tenth the size and settles the same way.
  • This is research, not advice.
