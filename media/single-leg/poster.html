Calls and puts, one at a time. What the decade actually says.

SPY, 2,689 sessions, January 2016 to September 2026. Two questions, asked separately, because they have opposite answers. Everything is priced off the VIX close of the session the trade opened on, with five cents a leg in costs, and every configuration is run at all 21 possible start dates and averaged so the answer is not the luck of where the calendar fell.

✕

Buying a call or a put off the band

No edge. Not a small one — none. Stop looking here.

  • Searched 576 combinations: 8 entry rules × 2 directions × 4 targets × 3 stops × 3 time limits.
  • Entry is the open of the next session, never the close that fired the signal. A bar touching both levels is scored as the stop.
  • Against taking the trade anyway: 292 of 576 beat their own control. That is 50.7% — a coin flip.
  • Against a placebo that reruns the whole search on dates no rule chose: the real best edge is 0.51 R, the noise search averages 0.57 R, and 64% of noise searches did better.
  • Out of sample on 2022 onward: 4 of 14 survived. Ranked on raw profit 13 of 20 looked fine — all 13 were the market's drift wearing a rule's clothes.
✓

Selling one at the band

A real, measurable edge — and it is the same variance premium, in two legs instead of four.

  • Sell one call 1.25σ above the market, buy one 0.5σ further up. Two options, same type, one order.
  • 96.9% of months win. The payoff demands 86.5%. The gap — 10.4 points — is the entire edge.
  • +5.3% of the money at risk per month. Positive in all 11 years and all 21 start dates.
  • Loses the full maximum in 0.4% of months — only three market episodes in the decade could produce one (Dec 2017, Jul 2022, Oct 2023), and most start-date calendars caught none or one.
  • No entry filter improves it. VIX high, VIX low, nothing: the edge is in the calendar, not a signal.

The law you cannot get around

On a path with no edge, a target T and a stop S are reached first with probability S/(T+S) and T/(T+S). Win rate and reward-to-risk are welded together. A 90% win rate forces a reward near 1-to-9. A 3-to-1 reward forces a win rate near a quarter.

There is no trade in this study — none — with a high win rate and a high reward-to-risk. Asking for both is asking for an edge over that line, and the only honest thing to do is measure the gap. That gap is what you are paid.

Why the second leg is not optional

Sell the call on its own and the worst month of the decade looks like this:

Naked put, Feb–Mar 2020−92.70 pts As a multiple of the credit72× Margin grew to3.7× Broker called onday one

The same month with the second leg: −7.53 points, twelve times smaller, and exactly the number printed on your screen when you opened it.

A backtest that settles every trade on the terminal close cannot see a margin call. That is why it is measured separately rather than assumed away.

How you actually do it
STEP 1
Pick a day, any day

Once a month, the same day each month. There is no setup to wait for — filtering by VIX, high or low, changed nothing. Twelve decisions a year.

STEP 2
Measure the move

Take the index level and the VIX close. One sigma is index × (VIX ÷ 100) × √(days ÷ 365), using calendar days to expiry.

STEP 3
Two strikes

Sell the call 1.25σ above. Buy the call 0.5σ further up. Round to listed strikes. Send both legs as one order at a net credit limit — never leg in.

STEP 4
Size it

Max loss is the gap between strikes minus the credit, known exactly at entry. Buy the number of contracts that makes it 5% of your account. Not margin, not notional.

STEP 5
Then do nothing

You never sell. Hold to expiry. No stop, no adjustment, no profit target. It cash-settles by itself. Hold-to-expiry is the only rule the decade measures.

The fill floor. The model collects about 11% of the strike gap as credit. If you cannot fill above 5.6%, there is no edge left — cancel and skip the month. Sizing. One SPX spread risks real money; XSP is the same index at one tenth the size and settles the same way, so divide every dollar figure by ten. What this is not. Every premium here is a model's, not a broker's quote, which is why step 3 has a floor. Eleven years is eleven years, not a guarantee. This is research, not advice.
