The core problem

Every losing trade is the contract expiring worthless — −99%, every time. The table below uses the recommended +10% target, where each winner pays enough that a loss costs about a dozen trades. At your +3% target the same loss costs 57 winners.

At your +3% target the break-even loss rate is 1.77% — one loss in 57 trades. You are running about 2.1%. That gap is small, invisible over three days, and it is the entire difference between compounding and bleeding.

Why your friend's record proves nothing

Three winning days is what a losing strategy looks like

With a 98% win rate, a run of winners is the expected outcome even when the expectancy is negative.

Four months with one loss is roughly a 1.2% loss rate — slightly better than break-even, on a sample far too small to tell the two apart. Your friend is not proving the edge; he is inside the streak that any 98% strategy produces.

The fix

A bigger target buys cushion

The loss is always about −99%, whatever your target. So the only lever is how much each winner pays. Raise the target and the break-even loss rate rises far faster than your actual loss rate does.

At +3% you have about a point of margin. At +10% you have 5.4 points. That is the difference between a strategy that survives a bad month and one that does not.

Position size

Never all-in on this

At 100% of the account a single loss takes 98.8% and the strategy compounds to −100%. The arithmetic is not close. 10–20% is the working range: enough to compound, small enough that one bad afternoon is a setback rather than the end.

The recommendation

    What to expect

    Honest limits

    What this cannot tell you

    The 15:00 entry skips 43% of days. On those sessions no contract sits near $2 with an hour left. One skipped day had a 9.9% index move — a crash the rule dodged by accident of pricing, not by design.

    Seven losses is a thin basis for a tail estimate. The loss rate could be double what the sample shows and you would not see it for a year.

    Nothing predicts the losing days. Their move size, range and direction are statistically identical to the winners. No filter tested removed them.

    This is a positive-expectancy bet with a fat left tail, not a safe income stream. Treat every trade as one that might return nothing at all, and size so that outcome is survivable.
