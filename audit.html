Correction issued

The data is sound, and he is not making prices up

An earlier version of this audit claimed half his exit prices were impossible. That finding was wrong, and it is worth stating plainly before anything else.

What the re-check found

Every alert was matched to a contract and his quoted entry price tested against that contract’s own high–low range on the day. 98% of his entry quotes fall inside the real range, and we selected the nearest listed expiry 97% of the time — his “ticker and price only” posts mean the front contract, which is what we priced. A true same-day expiry only existed for 18% of those alerts, because most tickers list weekly expiries, not daily ones.

On exits: 79% of his quoted exit prices did trade that day. Only 21% are above the contract’s high and genuinely impossible. The gap is timing, not invention — he quotes the day’s best print, posted at a moment when the contract sat lower. The clearest case was MU on 13 August: he posted $11.35 → $34.00, the earlier audit called it fabricated because the price at his post was $17.30, but the contract’s actual high that day was $42.53. His number was real.

What this changes: his credibility, not the strategy results. Every backtest below walks real 1-minute bars from a verified entry and never uses a posted exit. A follower still captures a median +45% where he quotes +75% — that is the honest number to plan around.

Channel by channel

Each feed optimised on its own, then tested on data it never saw

Every channel got its own search over entry window, premium cap, calls-versus-puts and exit rule — roughly 55,000 combinations in total. Rules were chosen on the first 70% of each channel’s history and scored on the final 30%, which selection never touched. Lottery rules were excluded up front: a rule needs a 30% win rate and cannot have three trades supplying more than 60% of its profit.

The column that matters is all viable rules. On volume-alerts, the entire field of 21,267 rules averages +1.71% per trade on the holdout — you did not need to pick the right one. On swing and day-trades, both the best decile and the field are negative out of sample. That is the difference between an edge and a good backtest.

Trade frequency

The filter was costing you two thirds of your trades

Earlier versions of this audit ran a first-10-minutes, sub-$5 filter inherited from an old result. On the holdout, loosening it raises both frequency and return — the filter was removing good trades. Measured on the holdout period only.

This is one trade per day by construction — the first alert after the open, nothing else. Volume-alerts tops out near 3.7 a week including puts. If you are seeing five a week you are counting other channels, and those did not survive the holdout test.

The moonshots

The +500% calls are real. They are also unpredictable.

Across all 2,155 audited alerts, measured to expiry.

Nothing at entry predicts them

Premium paid, time of day, calls versus puts, market direction, VIX level — every feature tested returned a lift between 0.7× and 1.2× against the 10.4% base rate. Cheap contracts are not more likely to run. There is no filter that finds them.

But speed, once you are in, does

The only real signal is how fast it moves in the first ten minutes.

The strategy

One trade a day: the first alert he posts in volume-alerts after the 9:30 open, taken only if it is a call. No time-of-day filter, no price cap — every attempt to narrow the entry cost more in missed trades than it gained in quality. His puts are skipped: including them adds 1.9 trades a month and costs 2.4 points of monthly return.

    Why trust it

    It holds in every period tested independently, and the holdout is better than the window it was chosen on — the opposite of what an overfit rule does.

    Projection

    What your account does

    Enter a starting balance. Outcomes are resampled from the 266 real audited trades, 4,000 simulated runs per horizon, compounding at your chosen position size.

    Full-record average is 14.0 a month, about 3.2 a week.

    Median is the middle outcome, not a promise. The p25–p75 band holds half of all simulated runs — a quarter of the time you finish below the p25 figure. Assumes 2% round-trip slippage. Deliberately stops at twelve months: beyond that, compounding arithmetic stops being a forecast, because the alert flow, the edge and your own position size all change.

    Sensitivity

    The two dials that decide everything

    Slippage — the unmeasured input

    Every figure on this page assumes 2% round trip. It has never been measured on your fills, and it is the single largest source of error here.

    Position size

    Growth keeps rising to about 75% of balance because the stop bounds each loss near 20% — but that assumes every stop fills. 25% is the recommendation: it gives up some growth to survive a stop that gaps.

    Track record

    Every month the rule has existed

    Compounded at 25% per trade. Bars show the month’s result; the rule goes quiet for whole months when he posts nothing that qualifies.

    Nine of the last twelve months had three or fewer qualifying alerts. The setup is drying up: his median premium has risen from $0.65 to $2.70 since 2023 and he posts later in the session than he used to. Plan on roughly four trades a month, not seven.

    Limits

    What this cannot tell you

    Slippage is unmeasured. At 2% the strategy returns about 9% a month. At 5% it returns roughly 4%. Twenty logged fills would settle it, and nothing else on this page moves the answer as much.

    He does not post every day. There is a first alert on 622 of 831 weekdays — 75% of trading days. If he posted one every session the same rule would return about +25% a month instead of +16%. That missing quarter is the largest remaining lever on this strategy and it is entirely his, not yours.

    $150,000 in three months is not reachable. That needs 15× in a quarter. This compounds about 1.3×. The same edge reaches it in roughly two and a half years from $20,000 — the timeline was the error, not the strategy.

    Past behaviour, not a forecast. Every number is resampled from what already happened. A signal service can change or stop at any time.
