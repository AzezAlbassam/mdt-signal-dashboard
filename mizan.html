01 — the rule he gave away in full

The numeric ladder

Posted free on 24 December 2023 as a two-part lesson. It is the most completely specified thing on his account, and it is three lines of arithmetic:

بنشتغل على آخر موجة قمتها ٤٦٠٦ وقاعها ٤١٠٣ وبتكون المعادلة كالتالي ( مجموع القاع * ٤٥ ) — القاع ٤١٠٣ ٤+١+٠+٣ = ٨ — ٨ *٤٥ = ٣٦٠ — نضيف ٣٦٠ للقاع مرتين ونرسم الترندات

We work on the last wave, peak 4606 and low 4103. The equation is (digit sum of the low × 45). The low 4103: 4+1+0+3 = 8. 8 × 45 = 360. We add 360 to the low twice and draw the trends.

His anchors are real prints. SPX bottomed at 4103.78 on 27 October 2023 and peaked at 4607.07 on 27 July 2023. He is reading genuine swing extremes. The arithmetic reproduces exactly — try it:

02 — why the flagship cannot be ported

The angles are a property of the picture

On 7 October 2024 he posted a Tadawul chart with two trendlines from one origin, labelled 51° and 24°, and the rule written across it in yellow:

51 % 2.125 = 24

Measure the angle of the rally leg, divide by 2.125, draw a second line from the same origin at the result. That line is the support.

A chart angle is atan(Δprice × pxPerPrice ÷ Δbars × pxPerBar). It describes the picture, not the prices. Reconstructing his chart's pixel scale reproduces his figures exactly — his own numbers are internally consistent. The trouble is that "his chart's scale" is his monitor. Drag the slider:

He knows this, which is why the setup post is emphatic: ريست للشارت ← الإعدادات ← المقاييس والخطوط ← قفل نسبة السعر — reset, settings, scales and lines, lock price ratio — and the Tadawul post specifies the daily frame بدون تصغير او تكبير, with no zooming. But locking the ratio freezes whatever is on screen at that moment. It does not define a scale anyone else shares.

Consequence for the indicator. Pine Script sees prices and bar indices, never pixels. There is no faithful port of an angle rule — any version would have to invent a price-per-bar convention and would then be drawing that invention rather than his method. This is not a limit of effort or of Pine. The quantity is not in the data.

03 — the measurement

Neither rule beats a line drawn at random

His claim is precise and therefore testable: زيرو انعكاس ومن أول لمسة — price turns at the level, from the first touch, without penetrating it. That splits into two numbers per touch: how far past the level price went, and how far it then travelled back.

A hit rate alone means nothing, because any line in a moving market gets touched and is followed by movement. So every rule is scored against a matched null: same anchors, same scoring, same thresholds, with only the level price randomised.

Numeric ×45 — vs random multiplier3463193.8%4.3%0.631

2,943 SPX sessions, 2015–2026. Nothing clears p = 0.05 at any of the four thresholds tested. The sharpest test is the last row: rebuild his ladder with a random constant in place of 45, hold everything else fixed, and it scores slightly better than 45 does. The multiplier carries no information.

A false positive worth recording. The first version of this test displaced null levels uniformly around the anchor, so half landed below it and were touched on the next bar — a different regime entirely. Against that broken null the numeric rule "beat chance" at p = 0.003. Fixing the null made the effect vanish. The null model is the experiment.

05 — what this site already knew

You have measured his advice once before

The $2 SPX study on this site tests a strategy that is his, posted 6 September 2024: in SPX the best out-of-the-money strikes are priced 3 to 4; split $1,000 across four contracts at a strike priced 2.5.

Measured over 655 sessions: a 98.1% win rate and −0.12% per trade — negative, because the 1.9% of losses cost −99% each. What turned it positive was a later entry and a larger target. That fix came from measurement, not from geometry.
