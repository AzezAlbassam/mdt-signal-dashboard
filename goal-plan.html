01Why capital matters more than aggression

Your $2,500 needs 60× to reach $150k; $20,000 would need only 7.5×. Capital does more for your odds than any amount of extra risk, and it is the one variable fully under your control — so if you can add to the stake later, that beats sizing up per trade.

03When do you close a winner?

This is the rule the 74% depends on, so it is worth being exact: you hold until MDT calls the exit. Your only unilateral action is the −30% stop. Capping your own upside breaks the maths, because the profit in his record is concentrated in a small number of large winners.

Closing every trade at +30% takes the goal to 0% and pushes ruin to 19%. Your losses stay at −30% (−37% after slippage) while your wins are capped at +30% — a coin flip with worse-than-even payoff. Tight profit-taking is right for a small preservation-focused stake; it is fatal when the objective is 7.5×.

If you cannot watch for his exit, the workable mechanical substitute is sell ⅓ at +50%, ⅓ at +150%, ride the last ⅓ — 57% to goal with 1% ruin.

04The strategy, exactly

  1. Start with $2,500 — a 25% shot at $150k and no wipeout risk. Adding capital is the cheapest way to raise those odds: $5,000 makes it 37%, $10,000 makes it 55%, $20,000 makes it 71%. Doubling the stake does more than any change to how you trade.
  2. Risk 50% of the balance per trade — never 100%. All-in adds 5 points of hit-rate and brings back a 7% chance of losing three quarters of the account. Half-balance sizing cannot be wiped out by a single trade.
  3. Hard stop at −30%, entered as a live order at fill. Without a stop the geometric mean of his return pool is −100%: one total loss ends a compounding account permanently. With the stop it is +18% per trade. This rule is the strategy.
  4. One position at a time. Half your balance per trade means at most two open; keep it to one so the stop stays enforceable and you are never forced to sell the wrong leg.
  5. Exit when he exits — not on a fixed profit target. The only number you enforce yourself is the −30% stop. See section 03.
  6. Take the day's first clean alert. No cherry-picking — profit is concentrated in a handful of trades, and selectively skipping is how followers miss them.
  7. Do not bank profits during the challenge. Tested every variant: at 50% sizing your ruin risk is already zero, so skimming only starves the compounding — banking 30% every 10 wins cuts your odds from 43% to 28% and leaves the downside slightly worse. Let it ride.
  8. Stop the challenge if the account halves. Pre-committed, not decided in the moment.
  9. Review at 20 closed trades against a 70% win rate. That is the break-even line after slippage and commissions. Below it, this loses money no matter how good the alerts look.
