You were right

There is no spread problem on SPY

I previously measured this on single-stock contracts and got 3.2%. That was the wrong instrument. On SPY the quote is genuinely penny-wide all session, and the at-the-money contract stays around a dollar, so the toll is under 1.5% at every hour you might enter.

Measured from real NBBO quotes: the bid/ask is $0.01 from open to close. Your objection was correct and my earlier Plan A conclusion was wrong.

The real obstacle

The contract bleeds while you hold it

What an at-the-money 0DTE call does if you simply buy it at 14:00 ET and wait.

The first few minutes are a coin flip — median move zero, green under half the time. After that decay takes over hard: −19% by half an hour, −21% by an hour. On 0DTE theta is not a slow drip, it is the dominant force within ten minutes. There is no quiet window to scalp in.

An earlier version of this page showed a positive first five minutes. That was a measurement error: it entered at the bar’s open, a price already gone by the time you could act. Entering at the close of the bar you actually decided on removes it entirely.

Verdict

What to take from this

Your two corrections were both right. These are options, not stock, and SPY has no meaningful spread. My earlier Plan A analysis was measuring the wrong instrument and reached the right answer for the wrong reason.

The +3% target still fails, but now for a reason you can act on: your stop is wider than your target on a decaying instrument. Reverse that and the arithmetic changes.

Do not confuse a rising market with an edge. Every positive number here comes from SPY going up. Pull that out and the structure costs about 1% a trade, every trade, forever.

If you want SPY exposure, this is an expensive way to buy it. The same directional view expressed in shares or longer-dated calls does not pay theta by the minute.
