Plan A · feasibility study · $1,000 start

Can you make 3–6% a day?

The plan is one trade idea a day, banking 3–6% and stopping. This works out what that would require, and how likely it is, using 547 real option day-trades as the measuring stick.

3% a day, compounded for a year
1,718×

$1,000 would become $1,717,832. At 6% a day it becomes $2.4 billion. Nothing in markets compounds like that, so the target is not aggressive — it is outside the range of possible outcomes. What follows is the honest version of the same plan.

The arithmetic

Small daily percentages are enormous annual ones

This is the part that makes the plan feel reachable: 3% sounds modest. Compounded over 252 trading days it is not.

For scale: the best hedge funds in history compound at roughly 2–3% a month. A 1%-a-day trader would be the best performer alive.

The requirement

What edge would 3% a day actually take?

Working backwards. To make 3% in a day from n trades at a given position size, each trade has to carry a certain expected return. Compared against the best per-trade edge measured anywhere in this project — +4.98%, from 547 audited, heavily filtered option trades.

The rows marked achievable are a trap. They only work by taking 5–10 trades every day. That +4.98% edge was measured on about 14 trades a month — the pick of the crop. Force ten trades a day and roughly 470 of your 500 annual trades are not that setup at all. Edge and frequency are not independent: the marginal trade is always worse than the average one.

The outcomes

Where you land depends almost entirely on your edge

$1,000, 252 days, 2 trades a day, 20% of the account per trade. The only thing changing between rows is the per-trade edge.

Win rate and reward:risk vary across the rows; the size of a winning trade is held at the audited +24% throughout, so only the edge is changing.

Look at the break-even row. A trader with a genuinely zero edge — not bad, just average — still ends the year down 67% of the time, with a median of $708 and a 67% drawdown along the way. Nothing was done wrong; that is pure volatility drag from risking 20% a trade. Being break-even at the trade level is a losing strategy at the account level. Most retail day traders are below that line once spreads are counted, which is the row ending at $181.

Your progression idea

Does banking a 30% gain and easing off help?

Your suggestion: once up 30%, trade smaller for a while, then reset. Tested against flat sizing on identical trade sequences.

It does not add return — it slightly reduces it, because you are sizing down exactly when the account is compounding fastest. What it does buy is a smoother ride, which matters if a drawdown would make you quit. Treat it as a psychological tool, not a performance one.

Verdict

What to do with this plan

3% a day is not a target, it is a category error. It implies 1,718× a year. No instrument, desk or fund produces that. The plan cannot fail from bad execution because it cannot succeed with good execution.

The daily target itself also works against you. Stopping at +3% caps your best days while your losing days run to the daily stop. You truncate the right tail and keep the left one — the opposite of what the edge needs.

What is real: at the audited edge and 20% sizing, the median year takes $1,000 to roughly $110,000 — and that already assumes you find 500 genuine setups a year, which nothing in the data supports.

The honest version of this plan is a monthly target, not a daily one, sized so a six-loss streak does not end you. Plan B is the better-shaped idea — same capital, but the maths works differently.
