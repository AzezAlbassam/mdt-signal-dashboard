Why grabbing 5% doesn’t work

The plan is to take a 2–3 DTE call, grab a quick 3–7% and close. Tested on 547 real option trades — actual contracts, not stock. It loses money, and the reason is one number most traders never subtract.

A +5% scalp, after the spread
−3.17%

Per trade. Not because the option fails to move — it reaches +5% on 51% of trades. The move is real; the profit isn’t, because a 5-cent spread on a $1.55 contract costs 3.2% every round trip.

The trap

The option moves. You just don’t keep it.

Hit rate is high for small targets — that part of your intuition is right. What kills it is that the spread is a fixed toll, and a small target has almost nothing left after paying it.

Read the gross and net columns together. A +5% target is hit on half of all trades and is break-even before costs — there is no edge there to begin with. After a normal spread it is −3.17%. The edge in these contracts lives in the big moves; taking 5% means paying full toll for a fraction of the ride.

The mechanism

The spread decides everything

The same trades, priced at different round-trip costs. Watch how differently the small and large targets respond.

A +5% target pays the toll and keeps 1.8%. A +40% target pays the identical toll and keeps 36.8%. That is the whole argument: the cost is per trade, not per percent. Even with a perfectly free spread, the +5% scalp still only breaks even — so tighter execution does not rescue it.

Where the break-even point sits

Below roughly +20% on the contract, you cannot clear a normal spread.

Verdict

Drop this one

The small-target scalp is the single clearest rejection in all this work. It is not marginal or unproven — it is negative gross, before you pay anyone anything. Every variant from +3% to +15% loses after a normal spread.

Your reasoning was half right. Options do move 5–7% constantly, and you do hit those targets about half the time. The error is assuming a hit equals a profit.

The fix is not a better entry, it is a bigger target. Same contracts, same signals, same spread — take +40% instead of +5% and the identical trade goes from −3.17% to +2.44%.

That is Plan B. Fewer trades, longer waits, far more per trade. The patience you identified as the key variable turns out to be the only one that matters.
