What this model does and does not do

It compounds three things: the dividend per share at your growth rate, the share price at your price-growth rate, and the share count from contributions plus reinvested dividends. Contributions are added monthly and buy shares at the average price for that year.

By default it does not hold your growth rate flat. Growth runs at the rate you set for five years, then fades linearly to 5% a year by year twenty, and the share price fades on the same schedule. This matters enormously: a 20% grower held flat for two decades produces a number roughly four times larger than the faded one, and the flat number is close to fiction. Companies decelerate as they get big — that is the whole reason this screen hunts small ones. You can switch the fade off to see the difference.

It still assumes no recession, no dividend cut, no valuation change beyond the price growth you set, and no trading costs. Real twenty-year outcomes are lumpier than this in every direction. The value of the projection is in the comparison between assumptions, not in the final digit.

One check worth watching: if you set price growth well below dividend growth, the implied yield climbs every year, and a company whose yield reaches 15% is one the market has decided will cut. The banner above warns you when the assumptions stop being coherent.

Research and analysis only — not financial advice. Projections are arithmetic applied to assumptions you choose, not forecasts. Dividends can be cut, and companies that raised for twenty years have stopped in the twenty-first.
