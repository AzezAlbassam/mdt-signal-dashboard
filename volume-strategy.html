The verdict in one paragraph

Nine out of ten alerts in this channel never get a result posted. The one in ten that do are 99% winners. So the entire outcome of your $2,000 depends on a single unknown: what happened to the 4,332 alerts nobody ever mentioned again.

If they were mostly never-taken watchlist notes, $2,000 becomes an impossible number. If even 7–10% of them were real trades that lost, $2,000 goes to zero. The channel gives you no way to tell which. That is not a strategy with unknown returns — it is a strategy with an unknown sign.

04If you do it anyway — the structure that survives

You asked when to take money off the table so a blow-up can't take you to zero. Given that the sign of this edge is unknown, the structure below is the answer: it makes the worst case "I lost the $2,000 I set aside" instead of "I lost the $2,000 and everything it made".

  1. Treat the $2,000 as spent the moment you send it. Not risk capital — expendable capital. Nothing in this analysis supports sizing it as an investment.
  2. Bet a fixed $100–200 per alert, not a percentage of the balance. Fixed-dollar sizing caps how fast a losing streak compounds. This is the single change that most reduces ruin.
  3. Pull the original $2,000 out the moment the account first doubles. From then on you are playing only with profit, and 100% drawdown of capital becomes impossible. In every scenario where this strategy works at all, banking the stake costs you almost nothing.
  4. Skim half the profit at every subsequent double ($4k → bank $1k, $8k → bank $2k, …). This is the "divide the total" rule you were reaching for.
  5. Stop after 20 alerts and compare against the channel. If your realised results are materially below its posted median of +148%, you have measured the gap between the highlight reel and reality — and you should stop.
